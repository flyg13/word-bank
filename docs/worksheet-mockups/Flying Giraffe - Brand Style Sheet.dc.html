Flying Giraffe

Brand style sheet

The screen system behind the teacher interface — colour, type, components, motion and voice. Print rules for the worksheet itself are a separate, stricter contract; see section 07.

01 — Principles
Sound measured, not sold

Credibility comes from precision. Concrete numbers where they are true — 10mm rows, 15×10mm boxes, sixteen checks — beat adjectives every time.

Colour never carries meaning alone

Typography carries it; colour reinforces. Every state must survive greyscale and colour-blind reading.

Delight lives on screen only

Gradients, motion and the flying giraffe belong to the app. Paper stays quiet, black-and-white safe, and geometrically exact.

One screen, one decision

No wizards, no tier language, no time promises. Charge at download, explain at the moment it matters.

03 — Typography

Atkinson on screen, Andika on paper

Two typefaces, one job each — and the boundary is the same one everything else in this document respects. Atkinson Hyperlegible runs the interface: Braille Institute, drawn for low vision, tighter in chrome and with published provenance. Andika runs every worksheet: SIL, drawn for literacy learners, single-storey a matching how a child is taught to write. Andika is embedded in the worksheet file and verified by an automated check, so a missing font is a build failure rather than a silent substitution.

05 — Logo & motion

The coin and the giraffe

The coin is the mark: 104px on entry and confirmation, 44px in headers and dialogues, 28–34px as a favicon-scale lockup. Clear space equals a quarter of its diameter. Never recolour it, never place it on the holographic wash at header size, never crop it. The wordmark sits beneath or beside at 12–15px, uppercase, .26–.34em tracking.
The illustrated giraffe is the waiting-state mascot and appears nowhere else. Motion vocabulary: a 13s drift across the panel, 0.95s wing beat, 1.9s bob, two slow somersaults late in each pass. Easing is always gentle cubic-bezier(.4,.05,.6,.95); nothing bounces, nothing spins fast, nothing pulses under text. All motion honours prefers-reduced-motion and no motion ever appears on a worksheet.
06 — Voice

Plain, specific, unhurried

Write like someone who measured, not someone selling. Australian English, active voice, sentences a tired teacher reads once.

Say
“We check the things you can't see.”
“Room to write. 10mm rows, 15×10mm answer boxes.”
“Sixteen checks run on every sheet before you see it.”
“Same questions, same numbers, no prompts.”
“Set scale to 100%. Turn off ‘fit to page’.”
measured · checked · verified · room to write · page features · access
Avoid
revolutionary · game-changing · AI-powered as a headline
cure · fix · struggling learners · special needs children
Say children with dyslexia or neurodivergent learners.
Any outcome promise. Any time promise — no countdowns, no “in 30 seconds”.
Passive constructions. Tier or plan language in the flow.
07 — Paper is a different contract

What renders is what the child receives

None of the screen styling above crosses onto a worksheet. Paper follows the artboard: fixed geometry, black-and-white safe, no gradient, no shadow, no rounded card, no brand colour.

A4, 12 slots of 22mm — every block declares a slot count from {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}. Placement is deterministic.
A page break falls only between blocks — never inside a question. This is the founding constraint, not a preference.
11pt body on a 180mm content width — Andika, embedded and assertion-verified.
Writing space from OT guidance — 10mm rows, 15×10mm answer boxes, 8mm graph steps, 12mm bar widths. Where a minimum is reasoned rather than clinically validated, copy must not claim otherwise.
Content that doesn't fit is regenerated, not clipped — generate → render → measure → retry.
When a minimum and a slot count conflict, the slot count moves.
